## Summary

Across a portfolio of 10+ enterprise products, inconsistent design patterns, redundant components, and lack of shared standards created friction for users, designers, and engineers. Each product team operated independently, leading to duplicated effort, inconsistent experiences, and slower delivery.

### The Problem

- **Inconsistent user experiences** across products
- **Duplicated design and engineering work** — teams rebuilding similar components
- **Slow delivery** — lack of reusable patterns meant starting from scratch
- **Quality issues** — accessibility, performance, and usability gaps
- **Scaling challenges** — new designers onboarding without clear standards

### Goals

1. Establish shared design standards and governance model
2. Build reusable component library adopted across products
3. Improve design-to-development collaboration and handoff
4. Reduce rework and accelerate delivery
5. Ensure accessibility and quality across all products

### Constraints

- Distributed teams across multiple time zones
- Each product had different tech stacks and priorities
- Limited dedicated design systems resources
- Need to maintain product velocity during transition
- Existing products with legacy code and patterns

## Role & Scope

As Design Lead, I:

- Founded and led the Design Guild for the BeerTech and North America zone
- Established design standards, principles, and ways of working
- Collaborated with engineers to build Storybook component documentation
- Led governance model and adoption strategy
- Supported global design system rollout across 10+ products
- Mentored designers on design systems thinking and implementation

**Team:** 8+ product designers across multiple products, 4 front-end engineers, Design Systems team

## Outcomes

### Quantitative Impact

- **10+ products adopted** design system components
- **50+ reusable components** documented in Storybook
- **Reduced design-to-dev time** — designers report faster handoffs
- **Fewer implementation bugs** — standardized patterns reduce errors

### Qualitative Impact

- **Improved consistency** — users experience more predictable interfaces
- **Faster onboarding** — new designers and engineers ramp up more quickly
- **Better collaboration** — shared language between design and engineering
- **Higher quality** — accessibility and usability baked into components

## Process

### 1. Assessment & Vision

**Understanding the Problem**

Conducted audit across products to identify:

- Inconsistent patterns (buttons, forms, navigation, tables)
- Redundant components being built by multiple teams
- Accessibility and usability gaps
- Friction points in design-to-dev handoff

**Defining Vision**

Articulated vision for design systems:

- **Consistency** — predictable, cohesive experiences across products
- **Efficiency** — reusable patterns that accelerate delivery
- **Quality** — accessibility and usability standards baked in
- **Collaboration** — shared language and tools for design and eng

### 2. Design Guild Formation

**Why a Guild?**

Needed a cross-functional community to:

- Align on standards and principles
- Share knowledge and best practices
- Drive adoption across autonomous product teams
- Maintain design quality without centralizing design org

**Operating Model**

- **Weekly sync meetings** — share work, discuss challenges, align on standards
- **Monthly deep dives** — focused sessions on specific topics (accessibility, data viz, etc.)
- **Slack channel** — async collaboration and quick questions
- **Office hours** — dedicated time for 1:1 support and mentorship

**Guild Principles**

1. **Consistency over customization** — prefer shared patterns
2. **Contribution over consumption** — teams contribute back to system
3. **Documentation over tribal knowledge** — write it down
4. **Collaboration over silos** — work together across products

### 3. Design Standards & Documentation

**Establishing Standards**

Created documentation for:

- **Design principles** — guiding values for decision-making
- **Component guidelines** — when and how to use each component
- **Accessibility standards** — WCAG compliance requirements
- **Interaction patterns** — standard behaviors for common scenarios
- **Data visualization** — guidelines for tables, charts, and dashboards

**Tools & Resources**

- **Figma library** — shared components and styles
- **Storybook** — component documentation for engineers
- **Confluence wiki** — design standards and guidelines
- **Slack channel** — real-time collaboration and support

### 4. Component Library & Storybook

**Collaboration with Engineering**

Partnered with front-end engineers to:

- Identify most-used components across products
- Prioritize components for standardization
- Define component APIs and props
- Build Storybook documentation

**Component Documentation**

For each component, documented:

- **Usage guidelines** — when and how to use
- **Variants and states** — all configurations and interactions
- **Accessibility** — ARIA attributes, keyboard navigation, screen reader support
- **Code examples** — implementation snippets
- **Do's and don'ts** — visual examples of correct and incorrect usage

**Priority Components**

Phase 1 focused on highest-impact components:

- Buttons and CTAs
- Form inputs and validation
- Data tables (critical for enterprise apps)
- Navigation patterns
- Modal dialogs
- Loading states and feedback

### 5. Governance & Adoption

**Governance Model**

Established lightweight governance to balance consistency with autonomy:

- **Component proposals** — teams can propose new components or changes
- **Design review** — optional reviews for complex patterns
- **Contribution guidelines** — clear process for adding to system
- **Deprecation policy** — how and when to sunset old patterns

**Driving Adoption**

Used multiple strategies:

1. **Lead by example** — used system components in high-visibility projects
2. **Make it easy** — clear documentation and support
3. **Incentivize** — celebrated teams that adopted and contributed
4. **Remove barriers** — worked with eng to ensure components met product needs
5. **Track progress** — measured adoption across products

**Measuring Success**

Tracked:

- Number of products using design system
- Number of components adopted per product
- Design-to-dev handoff time
- Implementation bugs related to UI components
- Designer and engineer satisfaction

### 6. Scaling & Iteration

**Challenges**

- **Resistance to change** — some teams preferred existing patterns
- **Tech stack differences** — not all components worked across all frameworks
- **Maintenance burden** — keeping system up-to-date required ongoing effort
- **Prioritization conflicts** — product deadlines vs. system contributions

**Solutions**

- **Flexible implementation** — provided multiple versions for different tech stacks
- **Clear value prop** — emphasized time savings and quality improvements
- **Support and training** — office hours, documentation, and onboarding
- **Incremental adoption** — allowed teams to adopt at their own pace

## Design Decisions

### Component Library vs. Full Design System

Chose to focus on **component library + standards** rather than full design system initially:

- Lower lift to get started
- Easier to drive adoption
- Could iterate and expand over time

Planned to evolve into full design system (tokens, theming, etc.) once core components were adopted.

### Guild vs. Centralized Team

Chose **guild model** over centralized design systems team:

- **Pros:** Cross-functional collaboration, shared ownership, better adoption
- **Cons:** Slower decisions, requires facilitation, depends on voluntary participation

Guild model fit our distributed, autonomous team structure better than centralized model.

### Documentation in Storybook vs. Separate Site

Chose **Storybook for component docs** rather than separate documentation site:

- Engineers already used Storybook
- Single source of truth for design and code
- Interactive examples with live code
- Lower maintenance burden

## Collaboration

### Cross-Functional Partnership

- **Product Designers** — collaborated on standards, contributed components, drove adoption
- **Engineers** — built component library, created Storybook docs, reviewed for feasibility
- **Product Managers** — supported prioritization and adoption timeline
- **Accessibility Team** — ensured compliance with standards

### Communication & Alignment

- **Guild meetings** — weekly syncs to align on standards and progress
- **Design reviews** — optional reviews for complex patterns
- **Lunch & learns** — educational sessions on system topics
- **Newsletters** — monthly updates on new components and changes

## Learnings

### What Worked Well

- **Guild model** — cross-functional collaboration drove adoption better than top-down mandate
- **Start small** — focusing on core components first built momentum
- **Partnership with eng** — tight collaboration ensured components met real needs
- **Clear documentation** — thorough docs in Storybook reduced questions and errors

### What I'd Do Differently

- **Earlier executive sponsorship** — would have helped with prioritization and resources
- **Clearer metrics upfront** — should have defined success metrics earlier
- **More designer involvement** — some designers felt system was imposed rather than co-created
- **Better contribution process** — process for adding components was initially unclear

### Key Takeaway

Design systems aren't just about components—they're about culture. The Design Guild created a shared community and ownership model that made adoption feel collaborative rather than mandated. By focusing on solving real problems (inconsistency, duplicated work, slow handoffs) rather than enforcing consistency for its own sake, we built a system that teams actually wanted to use.

The impact isn't just faster delivery or fewer bugs—it's that designers and engineers now have a shared foundation and language that makes collaboration easier and more effective.
