## Background

Najaa is a health and wellness application designed to support individuals throughout
their recovery journey by providing structure, accountability, and access to supportive resoruces.  

### The Problem

At the time I joined, the product experience was limited to onboarding and a basic task and goal 
tracking flow. Users lacked access to meaningful resources, progress visibility, and deeper reflection 
tools. This resulted in high drop-off rates and frustration among new users. 

### Product Direction

To address retention and engagement challenges, the product roadmap focused on expanding Najaa into a 
more comprehensive recorvery platform. This included:
- **Learning experiences** to support mental and emotional wellness through guided content
- **A blocking feature** to help users avoid triggering or harmful content.
- **An expanded tracking and statistics experience** that allowed users to record.
vistories and setbacks, uncover behavior patterns, and reflect on progress over time.

### My Contribution

My main responsibility was to transform a basic task-based tracker into a more reflective, insight-driven 
expeirence that helped users understand their recovery patterns over time. I also contributed 
to the homepage UI rework, refining layout and hierarchy to ensure high-value features were more quickly accessible
to users. 

## Designing Reflective Tracking for Recovery

Basic tracking only allowed users to mark victories or setbacks, without capturing *why,* *how,* or *what they were feeling*.
This limited users' ability to understand patterns tied to emotions, triggers, or environment. 

Research across mental health and wellness apps showed that reflection-driven tracking, logging 
context and emotions, creates more meaningful self awareness and long-term value.

## Reflection

### What Worked Well

Expanding the tracking experience to capture emotional and contextual details helped transform simple check-ins into 
moments of self-awareness. Paired with the statistics experience, users were able to identify patterns over time rather 
than viewing victories and setbacks in isolation.

### What I'd Do Differently

While engagement and retention metrics showed positive results, I would invest more time in qualitative follow-ups such as 
interviews or diary studies, to better understand how users interpret their statistics over longer periods of recovery.
