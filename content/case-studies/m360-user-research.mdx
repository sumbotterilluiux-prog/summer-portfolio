## Summary

M360 is a B2B CRM platform used by field sales representatives, territory managers, and RTM Directors. Despite significant investment in features, user adoption was lower than expected and satisfaction scores were declining.

### The Problem

- Low feature adoption across the platform
- User complaints about complexity and friction
- Lack of clarity on which features provided real value
- Gap between what was built and what users actually needed

### Users

- Field sales representatives (daily users)
- Territory managers (weekly users)
- RTM Directors (strategic users)

### Constraints

- Large, distributed user base across multiple time zones
- Limited access to users due to operational demands
- Need to maintain business continuity during research
- Confidential sales data and workflows

## Role & Scope

As Lead Product Designer & Researcher, I:

- Designed and led the research initiative across all user levels
- Conducted 32 user interviews and contextual inquiries
- Facilitated workshop sessions with stakeholders
- Analyzed quantitative usage data from analytics
- Synthesized findings into actionable insights and recommendations

**Team:** Product Manager, Engineering Lead, 2 Stakeholder Representatives

## Outcomes

### Key Findings

- **80% of features were underutilized** — majority of users relied on 5-7 core functions
- **Major gap between importance and satisfaction** — features users needed most had lowest satisfaction scores
- **Workflow fragmentation** — users spent significant time switching between M360 and other tools
- **Onboarding gaps** — new users struggled to discover and understand advanced features

### Impact on Product Strategy

Research findings directly informed a 6-month product roadmap focused on:

1. Simplifying core workflows
2. Sunsetting or consolidating underutilized features
3. Improving high-value, low-satisfaction features
4. Reducing dependency on external tools

## Process

### 1. Research Planning

Started by defining research objectives with stakeholders:

- Understand current usage patterns and pain points
- Identify gaps between user needs and current features
- Evaluate feature importance vs. satisfaction
- Uncover opportunities for workflow improvement

Created a research plan that balanced depth with operational constraints.

### 2. Data Collection

**Quantitative Analysis**

- Analyzed 6 months of usage analytics
- Mapped feature adoption rates across user segments
- Identified drop-off points and error patterns

**Qualitative Research**

- Conducted 32 one-on-one interviews (45-60 minutes each)
- Observed 12 contextual inquiry sessions in field environments
- Ran 3 workshop sessions with power users

**Survey**

- Distributed importance vs. satisfaction survey (125 responses)
- Measured feature usage frequency and perceived value

### 3. Analysis & Synthesis

Used affinity mapping to identify patterns across:

- Pain points and frustrations
- Workarounds and hacks
- Unmet needs and feature requests
- Mental models and expectations

Created user journey maps for each role to visualize:

- High-friction moments
- Points of confusion
- Tool-switching patterns
- Opportunities for improvement

### 4. Key Insights

**1. Feature Overload**

Users felt overwhelmed by the number of features. Most relied on a small subset of core functions and avoided exploring additional capabilities.

> "I know there's a lot this system can do, but I don't have time to figure it all out. I just stick with what works."
>
> — Territory Manager, 3 years experience

**2. Misaligned Priorities**

Features that scored highest in importance (account management, contact tracking, activity logging) had the lowest satisfaction scores due to usability issues.

**3. Integration Gaps**

Users frequently switched to external tools for tasks that M360 theoretically supported, indicating trust and usability issues with existing features.

**4. Role-Based Needs**

Different roles had very different needs:

- **Field reps** needed speed and mobile-first design
- **Territory managers** needed visibility and reporting
- **Directors** needed strategic insights and trend analysis

## Design Decisions

### Prioritization Framework

Created a 2x2 matrix plotting importance vs. satisfaction to prioritize improvements:

- **High importance, low satisfaction** → immediate focus
- **High importance, high satisfaction** → maintain quality
- **Low importance, high satisfaction** → monitor
- **Low importance, low satisfaction** → consider sunsetting

### Recommendations Delivered

1. **Simplify core workflows** — reduce clicks and streamline most-used features
2. **Sunset underutilized features** — remove or consolidate features with less than 10% adoption
3. **Improve high-value features** — redesign account management, contact tracking, and activity logging
4. **Consolidate external tools** — bring frequently-used external workflows in-house
5. **Enhance onboarding** — guided tours and contextual help for new users

## Collaboration

### Cross-Functional Alignment

- **Product:** Worked closely to translate findings into roadmap priorities
- **Engineering:** Shared technical constraints that shaped recommendations
- **Sales Leadership:** Validated findings and supported user access
- **Analytics:** Partnered to combine quantitative and qualitative insights

### Stakeholder Communication

Delivered findings through:

- Executive summary deck for leadership
- Detailed research report for product team
- Workshop sessions to socialize insights
- Journey maps and personas for ongoing reference

## Learnings

### What Worked Well

- **Mixed methods approach** — combining analytics, interviews, and surveys provided comprehensive understanding
- **Role-based segmentation** — interviewing across all levels revealed important differences in needs
- **Quantitative validation** — usage data confirmed qualitative findings and added credibility

### What I'd Do Differently

- **Earlier stakeholder involvement** — more upfront alignment on research objectives would have smoothed adoption of findings
- **Continuous research** — establish ongoing research cadence rather than one-time deep dive
- **Faster iteration** — move from insights to prototyping more quickly to test solutions

### Key Takeaway

Research doesn't just validate assumptions—it challenges them. The gap between what we thought users needed and what they actually needed was significant. This project reinforced the importance of talking to users early and often, and using data to drive decisions rather than intuition alone.
